Four moves

What separates people who ship working tools from people who keep restarting chats. Each move has a prompt you can copy and adapt.

  1. Start with the goal, then brainstorm

    Tell the AI what students need to learn and the moment you want in the room. Then have it brainstorm three different ideas before it builds anything. Its first idea is usually the most boring one. Your goal, your moment, and your look are the spec. Six specific lines beat a document nobody reads.

    Show the prompt 
    I teach [subject] in grades [grade band]. Students need to [learning goal].
The moment I want: students [arguing over an answer, racing the clock, begging for one more round].
Brainstorm 3 very different game ideas, 2 sentences each. Make one of them weird. Don't write any code yet.

  2. Bring your taste

    Without direction, every AI app gets the same look: gradients, emoji icons, rows of rounded cards. Give it a reference that fits your content, like an era, a real object, or a screenshot you like. Say what you don't want, too.

    Show the prompts 
    Make it look like [a real thing: a 1930s field notebook, a game show set, a museum exhibit].
Pull the colors and type from that reference.
No gradients, no emoji icons, no rows of identical rounded cards.
Rebuild the app in that style without changing how it works. Give me the whole file.
    Here's a screenshot of a design I like. [attach it]
Describe its colors, type, layout, and mood. Then restyle my app to match without changing how it works.

  3. Small changes, test every time

    Bundle small fixes in one prompt. Give big changes, like a restyle or a new feature, their own prompt. Test after every prompt and save a copy that works. When something breaks, drop to one change at a time, and if a fix breaks two other things, start a fresh chat with your last good copy.

    Show the prompts 
    Here is my working file. Make these small changes:
1. [small change]
2. [small change]
3. [small change]
Don't change anything else. Give me the whole file and list what you changed.
    Something broke, so I'm starting over from the last working version of my classroom app. [paste the file]
In this chat I only want one change: [describe it]. Read the whole file before changing anything.
    Here is my working file. Replace only the CONTENT block with [number] items about [topic] for grades [grade band]. Don't change anything outside the CONTENT block.

  4. Break it, then report it like a witness

    Test like a 7th grader: mash buttons, click out of order, try a phone and the projector. When something breaks, say what you expected, what happened, and the steps to make it happen. Paste the red text from the console (F12, or Ctrl+Shift+J on a Chromebook).

    Show the prompts 
    Act as a 7th grader trying to break this app or game the score. List the 5 most likely ways a student could cause a bug or cheat in a real class. Then fix the top one, give me the whole file, and tell me in one sentence what you changed.
    Bug report for my classroom app.
What I expected: [expected]
What actually happened: [what happened]
Steps to make it happen: 1. [step] 2. [step] 3. [step]
Console error: [paste red text, or "none"]
Find the cause before changing anything. Explain it in one sentence, then give me the smallest fix that solves it.

  5. What you don't need

    React, APIs, "agentic workflows," or magic phrases. Opening with "you are a world-class senior developer" is mostly superstition. A clear goal and a real reference beat a flattering role every time.

Own the file

Your app should be one HTML file you can open, edit, host, and hand to a colleague. Try it now with the sample below.

Why one file?

The page, the style, and the code all live inside it. Paste the whole app into any AI chat, double-click to open it, email it, or upload it anywhere. No missing images, no broken links, and it works offline. Put this line in every build prompt: a single-file HTML app, no external libraries, no images from the web

Run it on your computer

  • Windows: double-click the file. If it opens in Notepad, right-click it, choose Open with, and pick Chrome or Edge.
  • Mac: double-click to open in Safari, or right-click, choose Open With, and pick Chrome.
  • Chromebook: open the Files app, go to Downloads, and double-click the file. It opens in Chrome.
  • iPad and phones: local HTML files are unreliable here. Use a hosted link instead.

To edit it yourself, open it in a plain text editor: Notepad, the Text app on a Chromebook, or TextEdit on a Mac (turn on "display HTML files as HTML code" in TextEdit's settings). Or paste the whole file into your AI chat.

Host it on GitHub Pages

  1. Make a free account at github.com.
  2. Click New repository. Give it a short name like classroom-apps and set it to Public.
  3. Click Add file, then Upload files. Drag in your HTML file and click Commit changes.
  4. Go to Settings, then Pages. Set the source to Deploy from a branch, choose main and / (root), and save.
  5. Wait a minute or two. Your app lives at yourname.github.io/classroom-apps/your-file.html

Public means public. Nothing with student names goes in a repository. A file named index.html becomes the front page, which is how this page works.

Upgrade yours or plan one

Brought a tool? Upgrade it. Starting fresh? Plan one. Either way, work with a partner and save a copy after every step that works.

  1. Give it a look

    Here is my classroom app. [paste the whole file]
Restyle it to look like [a real thing that fits the topic]. Pull the colors and type from that reference. No gradients, emoji icons, or rows of identical rounded cards. Don't change how it works. Give me the whole file.

  2. Break it and fix it

    Act as a student trying to break this app or game the score. List the 5 most likely ways a student could cause a bug or cheat in a real class. Then fix the top one, give me the whole file, and tell me in one sentence what you changed.

  3. Fix the next one

    Fix #[number] from that list. Don't change anything else. Give me the whole file and tell me in one sentence what you changed.

  4. Let students carry their results

    Add a results screen at the end: a read-only text box with a plain-text summary of the student's work and a Copy results button, so students can paste it into our LMS. Don't save or send anything. Give me the whole file.

Ready check

  
Five checks before a tool goes in front of students. Use it on every build.

  

    

      
    
        
  • It works. It survived the break-it test, and you fixed the problems a real class would hit.
    • 
        
  • Everyone can use it. Readable from the back row, strong contrast, works with only a keyboard, and nothing depends on color alone.
          
    Accessibility prompt
            
    

    Check this app for accessibility before students use it: text size and contrast, whether everything works with only a keyboard (Tab, Enter, arrow keys), whether anything depends on color alone, whether buttons and images have labels a screen reader can read, and whether animations can be turned off. Fix the 3 problems that would affect the most students. Give me the whole file and list what you fixed.
    
          
    • 
        
  • It's private. No names, logins, or email. It doesn't save or send anything. Your own offline tool can save on your computer if it uses codes and backs up to a file.
          
    Privacy prompt
            
    

    Before I share this with students, check it. List every place this file stores, sends, or loads anything: browser storage, network requests, external scripts, fonts, images, or forms. Remove anything that sends student data or loads from the internet, and give me the whole file. If there's nothing to remove, say so.
    
          
    • 
        
  • Someone else tried it. A colleague or a small group used it before the whole class did, and you fixed one thing from their feedback.
    • 
        
  • You know who it's for. Keep it teacher-facing if it holds student info, still breaks sometimes, or needs you standing there to explain it. It's student-ready when it passes every check above and a student can use it cold.
